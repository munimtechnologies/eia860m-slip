# Why coal retirements miss EIA's January plan: 2019, 2024, 2025, and a prediction for 2026

Made by MV2 of Munim, Inc. with AI agents, 2026-10-16. CC BY 4.0. Every number below comes from the page cited next to it. This is a desk analysis of public documents. No EIA-860M rows were diffed for it (see Limits).

## Summary

- In the years when coal retirements overshot EIA's January plan, most of the excess came from closures decided **during** the year. Examples are Bruce Mansfield, Gorgas, Plant Hammond and Vistra's Illinois plants in 2019, and Rush Island in 2024.
- In 2025 the sign flipped. The decisions made during the year all **deferred** closures. EIA's own accounting (2.6 retired + 4.8 postponed + 1.1 cancelled = 8.5 GW planned) leaves no room for retirements added during the year.
- So a January coal plan is not a forecast in either direction. It lists only dates owners have already reported, and decisions made during the year move the outcome by more than the plan's own size.
- **Stated in advance (2026-10-16):** EIA's first count of 2026 coal retirements will be **0.3-2.5 GW (central 1.3 GW)**, against a plan of 6.4 GW. That is 5-39% of plan, and below 2025's 2.6 GW. The reason: 4.4 GW (69%) of the 2026 plan has already been deferred by name since the plan was set.

## Plan against outcome

| Year | January plan (GW) | Retired (GW) | Ratio | Main cause of the gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## 2019: 287% of plan

The plan was 4.5 GW of coal, half of it Navajo ([EIA, Jan 2019](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=37952)). The final count was 12.9 GW (Electric Power Annual Table 4.6). Five decisions dated after the plan add up to about 6.5 GW, roughly 80% of the excess:

| Plant | MW (basis) | Decision | Retired |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bruce Mansfield 1-2, PA | 1,660 (PJM) | after PJM's 2018-11-08 list, which still showed 2021 | 2019-02 |
| Gorgas 8-10, AL | 1,063 (EIA) | announced 2019-02-20 | 2019-04 |
| Hammond 1-4 (and McIntosh 1), GA | 982 (EIA, state total) | IRP filed 2019-01-31, approved 2019-07-16 | 2019 |
| Bruce Mansfield 3, PA | 830 (nameplate share) | announced 2019-08-09 | 2019-11 |
| Vistra's Coffeen, Duck Creek, Havana and Hennepin, IL | 2,002 (EIA, state total) | announced 2019-08-21 | 2019-11/12 |

Sources: [EIA Electric Power Monthly update, Jan 2020](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/monthly/update/archive/january2020/), [FirstEnergy Solutions, 2019-08-09](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/firstenergy-solutions-accelerates-deactivation-of-bruce-mansfield-unit-3-300899441.html), [PJM TEAC 2018-11-08](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/committees-groups/committees/teac/20181108/20181108-generation-deactivation-notification.ashx), [Sierra Club on Gorgas](https://www.sierraclub.org/press-releases/2019/02/alabama-power-announces-retirement-plant-gorgas), [Georgia PSC, 2019-07-16](https://psc.ga.gov/site/assets/files/4279/media_advisory_for_7-16-19_gpc_irp_for_web.pdf), [Vistra 10-Q Q3 2019](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1692819/000169281919000018/vistra-20190930x10q.htm). The MW figures mix PJM, EIA and nameplate bases, so the 6.5 GW sum is good to about ±0.3 GW.

## 2024: 172% of plan

The plan was 2.3 GW. EIA named Seminole 1 (626 MW) and Homer City 1 (626 MW) ([EIA, Feb 2024](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=61425)). The final count was 3,958.6 MW. Rush Island (Ameren Missouri, about 1,178-1,195 MW) could not have been in the plan. The two named units plus Rush Island already exceed 2.3 GW. MISO had kept the plant running until transmission upgrades expected by mid-2025 ([Utility Dive, 2022](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ameren-missouri-coal-rush-island-miso-ferc/630226/)), but it closed on 2024-10-15. That is about 71% of the 1.66 GW excess. The second half of the year confirms it. At mid-year EIA showed 0.7 GW still scheduled ([EIA, Aug 2024](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=62864)); 1.87 GW actually retired in H2, and the 1.17 GW difference matches Rush Island.

## 2025: 32% of plan

From [EIA, 2026-04-13](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67427):

- **Retired:** 2.6 GW at four plants: Indian River 4, DE (410 MW, Feb); Cholla 1 and 3, AZ (383 MW, Mar); Intermountain 1-2, UT (1,800 MW, Oct-Nov); Prairie Creek 1, IA (15 MW, Dec). These add up to 2,608 MW.
- **Not retired:** 4.8 GW postponed to a later year and 1.1 GW cancelled. 2.6 + 4.8 + 1.1 = 8.5 GW, which is the planned figure in this article.
- **DOE Section 202(c) orders:** J.H. Campbell 1-3, MI (1,331 MW); Centralia 2, WA (670); R.M. Schahfer 17-18, IN (722); F.B. Culley 2, IN (90); Craig 1, CO (427). Together 3,240 MW, 55% of the 5.9 GW that did not retire. Centralia 2 is now to be converted to gas in 2028.
- **Voluntary delays:** Brandon Shores (MD), South Oak Creek (WI) and Comanche (CO), 2.2 GW combined.
- These named groups (5.4 GW) leave about 0.5 GW of the 5.9 GW unnamed.

Every one of the 2025 orders was issued after the January plan. Campbell's came on 2025-05-23. Centralia's (12-16), Schahfer's and Culley's (12-23) and Craig's (12-30) came days before each unit's planned date ([DOE 2025 orders](https://www.energy.gov/ceser/2025-doe-202c-orders)).

**One year, three plan figures.** EIA's start-of-year article gave 8.1 GW ([EIA, Feb 2025](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=64604)). Its February 2026 article gave 8.0 GW ([id=67206](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67206)) and its April 2026 article 8.5 GW. This repo scores against the start-of-year headline (8.1 GW, 32%); on 8.5 GW it would be 31%.

## 2026: what has changed since the plan, and a prediction

The plan is 6.4 GW of coal, from EIA's December 2025 inventory ([EIA, 2026-02-23](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67206)). The units EIA names (4,752 MW) are below. Status is as of 2026-10-16:

| Unit | MW (EIA) | Status since the plan | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| J.H. Campbell 1-3, MI | 1,331 | DOE order 202-26-39, to 2026-11-14 (renewed every 90 days since May 2025) | [DOE 2026 orders](https://www.energy.gov/ceser/2026-doe-202c-orders) |
| Cumberland 2, TN | 1,231 | TVA board voted 2026-02-11 to postpone; no new date | [TVA](https://www.tva.com/energy/our-power-system/coal/cumberland-fossil-plant), [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southeast/2026/02/13/858062.htm) |
| R.M. Schahfer 17-18, IN | 722 | DOE order 202-26-46, to 2026-12-18 | DOE |
| South Oak Creek 7-8, WI | 616 | We Energies moved retirement to end of 2027 (April 2026) | [RTO Insider](https://www.rtoinsider.com/129786-we-energies-defers-oak-creek-coal-units-retirement-through-2027/) |
| Craig 1, CO | 427 | DOE order 202-26-49, to 2026-12-25 | DOE |
| Comanche 2, CO | 335 | not checked | |
| F.B. Culley 2, IN | 90 | DOE order 202-26-47, to 2026-12-18 | DOE |

- Deferred by name: 4,417 MW, or 69% of the plan. DOE orders account for 2,570 MW of it, voluntary decisions for 1,847 MW.
- That leaves at most about 2.0 GW of the plan (Comanche 2 plus about 1.65 GW EIA did not name) that can retire on schedule.
- DOE has also put OUC's Stanton 1 (FL) under orders since 2026-06-04. We have not checked whether it was in the 6.4 GW.

**The court ruling.** On 2026-09-11 the D.C. Circuit vacated the first Campbell order (202-25-3), calling Section 202(c) "a narrow, last-resort backstop" (*Michigan v. DOE*, No. 25-1159; [National Law Review summary](https://natlawreview.com/article/michigan-v-doe-dc-circuit-rejects-does-use-federal-power-act-emergency-powers-block)). Challenges to the renewals are in abeyance. DOE has issued three more retirement-deferral orders since, for Schahfer, Culley and Craig.

**Prediction, stated 2026-10-16, before any 2026 total is published:**

1. EIA's first count of 2026 coal retirements (expected in a Today in Energy article around February 2027) will be **0.3-2.5 GW**, with a central estimate of **1.3 GW**. We hold this as an 80% interval.
2. That makes 2026 the second year in a row below plan, and **below 2025's 2.6 GW**, the least since 2010 by EIA's account.
3. The main way this fails: Campbell (1.33 GW) retires in 2026 after its order lapses on 2026-11-14, or other orders are not renewed in December. If that happens, we will say so next to the score.

It will be scored against EIA's first count, and then against the final Form EIA-860 for 2026 (Electric Power Annual Table 4.6, around September 2027). This file's commit date on GitHub is the record that the prediction came first.

## Limits

- **Plan membership** is inferred from dates and from EIA's own sums. It is not read from the December EIA-860M planned-retirement rows. A row-level diff using this repo's code on the PUDL table would check it.
- **2026 retirements so far** were not read. Some of the ~1.65 GW EIA did not name may already have retired or been deferred.
- **Capacity bases differ** across sources (EIA net summer, PJM, nameplate). EIA's figures are used wherever EIA gives one.
- **No court documents were read.** The 2026 ruling is taken from a law-firm summary. The 2024 Rush Island court ruling is taken from news reports.
- **Sample:** the 2019/2024 versus 2025/2026 contrast rests on four years. It describes those years; it is not a model.

## Prior art

EIA itself broke 2025 down into retired, postponed and cancelled capacity and listed the DOE-ordered plants (id=67427), and the 2025 section above relies on that. Trackers such as POWER's 202(c) log and SynMax's coal reports follow single plants. We found no published account that sets the plan-versus-outcome gap side by side across years, overshoot years and shortfall years alike, and attributes each to named decisions made during the year. We also found no stated-in-advance number for 2026 coal retirements based on the deferrals already on record. If you know of one, open an issue.
