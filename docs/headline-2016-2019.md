# EIA's start-of-year plans, 2016-2019: the earlier years

MV2 of Munim, Inc., 16 October 2026 (0.2.4). Made with AI agents working in MV2's name. Text CC BY 4.0, code Apache-2.0.

This extends the plan-vs-actual record in `headline-plans.md` and `headline-retirements.md`
(2020-2026) back to 2016. Each plan is what EIA's *Today in Energy* said developers or
operators planned for the year. Each actual is the **final** count, from Table 4.6 of the
Electric Power Annual (Form EIA-860, net summer capacity) for that year, read from EIA's
archive on 2026-10-16. Every row and its exact quote is in `data/eia_headline_plans.csv` and
`data/eia_headline_retirements.csv`.

**Pooled over 2016, 2018 and 2019:** 82.7 GW built against 81.7 GW planned (**101%**). By
technology: solar 103%, wind 110%, gas 101%.

**Compared with 2020-2024** (same final source, from `headline-plans.md`): 194.1 GW built
against 245.1 GW planned (**79%**). Every year from 2020 to 2024 fell short (67-97%), and
solar was the biggest gap (73% pooled).

So the gap between plan and build is new. In the three earlier years with a usable plan,
the headline total was close to what was built. In 2020-2024 it overshot every year. The
plans also changed: solar was 18% of the 2019 plan and 58% of the 2024 plan. Solar beat its
small plans in 2018 and 2019 and fell short of its large plans in every year after.

## Retirements: retired / planned

| year | technology | planned GW | plan published | final GW | final / plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2018 | coal | about 13 | 2018-01-22 | 13.30 | 102% |
| 2019 | total | 8.3 | 2019-01-10 | 20.06 | 242% |
| 2019 | coal | 4.5 | 2019-01-10 | 12.90 | 287% |
| 2019 | gas | 2.2 | 2019-01-10 | 4.29 | 195% |
| 2019 | nuclear | 1.5 | 2019-01-10 | 1.48 | 98% |

EIA's own counts confirm 2019: about 12.7 GW of coal retired (January 2020 estimate) and
"nearly 14,000 MW" (April 2020), against the 4.5 GW planned in January 2019.

**Coal, final / plan, by year:** 2018 102%, 2019 287%, 2020 180%, 2021 206%, 2022 109%,
2023 104%, 2024 172%. Pooled 2018-2024: 69.1 GW retired against 49.8 GW planned (**139%**).
Coal retirements met or beat the January plan in each of these seven years. 2025 is the
first year in this record (which starts in 2018) where they fell well short: 2.6 GW retired against 8.1 GW planned
(32%, first count; EIA's final 2025 table is not out yet).

**Total retirements, pooled 2019-2024:** 87.7 GW against 64.1 GW planned (**137%**).

## How first counts compare with finals

- **Additions:** 2016, 2018 and 2019 first counts (EIA's spring articles) were within 4% of
  the finals for every technology given (for example 2018 wind 6.6 → 6.87 GW, 2019 gas
  8.3 → 8.08 GW).
- **Retirements:** the first count of total 2018 retirements (18.7 GW) was 18% below the
  final (22.9 GW); put the other way, the final was 23% higher. Coal counts were close:
  2018 13.7 GW (January 2019 estimate) and 12.9 GW (March 2019) against 13.3 GW final;
  2019 12.7 GW (January 2020 estimate) against 12.9 GW final.

## Limits

- **Plan vintages differ.** The 2019 plan is from January, as in 2020-2026. The 2016 plan is
  from March 1, 2016. The 2018 total, wind and solar plans are from May 7, 2018, after January
  and February had been reported, so they are partly actual. Both give those plans a head
  start, so the 2016 and 2018 ratios lean towards 100%. The 2019 total alone, from a clean
  January plan, is still 98%.
- **2016 total is a bound.** The plan said "more than 26 GW", so 107% is the highest the ratio
  can be. (`headline.py` prints it as 107% without a mark, because its marks follow the actual,
  not the plan.)
- **2018 gas** uses the January 2018 plan (20 GW). The May article says "about 21 GW" (92%).
- **2019 gas** is derived from a rounded share (34% of 23.7 GW). The article itself names
  6.1 GW combined-cycle and 1.4 GW combustion-turbine as the main parts (7.5 GW, 108%).
- **2017 is missing.** The archived 2017 Table 4.6 page redirects to EIA's electricity home
  page, and no January 2017 headline plan was found. EIA's own 2018 article compared the 2017
  plans from its annual survey with what came online: 78% of 25.1 GW came online on schedule.
  That is an on-time share, not the same measure, so it is not in the table.
- **Small sample.** These are three years of plans and seven of coal retirements. They show
  a change, not a cause. Table 4.6 counts a generator in the year its addition or retirement
  is reported on Form EIA-860.
