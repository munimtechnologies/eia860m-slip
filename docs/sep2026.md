## P3: what failed

Of the 20 generators that left, 16 were on the Retired sheet in August, 1 on the Operating sheet and 3 on the Planned sheet. The 16 are on no sheet the changelog reads in September.

- **The size is unusual.** Since April 2017 (EIA changed the Retired sheet's scope in March 2017), only two months had more generators leave the Retired sheet: November 2024 (277, the gap in EIA's own file; all 277 were back the next month) and January 2024 (23, all at plant 1439). The plan's record was 6 in all of 2025 and 1 in January to August 2026.
- **They are spread out.** The 16 are at 15 plants (885/1 and 885/2 share one). In every other month since April 2017 except November 2024, the generators that left the Retired sheet were at no more than 2 plants.
- **What they are is not yet known.** The job's reader keeps only key, sheet, status, planned month and MW. From an earlier job (annual860-v1, August 2026 census): none of the 16 is among the 2,703 generators that only the monthly Retired sheet lists as retired in 2002 to 2025, so each is either listed as retired by the annual EIA-860 2025 file or one of the 56 generators on August's Retired sheet whose retirement year is not in 2002 to 2025 (7,324 on the sheet, 7,268 retired in 2002 to 2025).

A follow-up, [`docs/sepretired-plan.md`](sepretired-plan.md), fixes three predictions about these 16 before job sepretired-v1 reads them: whether any is listed on another sheet in September, whether at least 12 are listed as retired by the annual EIA-860 2025 file, and whether at most 3 are listed again in October 2026.

P3 is a bar about the file, not about EIA's intent. A failure says September's file is unusual against its own record; it does not say whether EIA removed these generators on purpose, corrected them, or left them out by mistake.

## P4: the September cohort

The month before, 170 generators (13,854.0 MW) were listed as planned to start operating in September 2026. September lists 57 of them as Operating (4,403.3 MW), 112 still on the Planned sheet (9,420.7 MW; 106 of them with a later planned month) and 1 on no sheet the changelog reads (30.0 MW). The share, 0.3353, is inside the band.

With September added, January to September 2026 pooled is 423 of 1,364 (0.310). The largest next-month cohort of 2026 so far is June's 216; September's 170 is the second largest. Whether December 2026 has the largest of the year is pre-registered in [`docs/dec2026-plan.md`](dec2026-plan.md).

## Limits

- One month, four bars. P4 is a statistical bar; P2 and P3 count events in the file and do not tell real removals from moves to the Canceled or Postponed sheet or gaps in a file.
- The September file was read as EIA served it on 23 October 2026. A later revision by EIA would not be reflected.
- Generators are not independent: one plant can have many generators that move together.
